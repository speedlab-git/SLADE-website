SLADE: Shielding against dual exploits in Large Vision-Language Models

Patch-level and global contrastive adversarial fine-tuning that makes CLIP-based LVLMs robust to gradient-based and jailbreak attacks, while keeping fine-grained detail.

Abstract

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have emerged as transformative tools in multimodal tasks, seamlessly integrating pretrained vision encoders to align visual and textual modalities. Prior works have highlighted the susceptibility of LVLMs to dual exploits (gradient-based and optimization-based jailbreak attacks), which leverage the expanded attack surface introduced by the image modality. Despite advancements in enhancing robustness, existing methods fall short in their ability to defend against dual exploits while preserving fine-grained semantic details and overall semantic coherence under intense adversarial perturbations. To bridge this gap, we introduce SheiLding against Dual Exploits (SLADE), a novel unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning scheme that enhances the resilience of CLIP-based vision encoders. SLADE's dual-level contrastive learning approach balances the granular and the holistic, capturing fine-grained image details without losing sight of high-level semantic coherence. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SLADE-equipped LVLMs set a new benchmark for robustness against dual exploits while preserving fine-grained semantic details of perturbed images. Notably, SLADE achieves these results without compromising the core functionalities of LVLMs, such as instruction following, or requiring the computational overhead (e.g., large batch sizes, momentum encoders) commonly associated with traditional contrastive learning methods.

How SLADE works

Align clean and adversarial views at two levels: patch and image

SLADE adversarially fine-tunes the CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder on ImageNet, without labels. After training, it replaces CLIP inside an LVLM such as LLaVA or OpenFlamingo, with the language model and projection layer kept frozen.

1

Generate an adversarial view

For each clean image \(I\), PGD (10 steps, ℓ∞) produces a perturbed image \(I'\) within radius ε:

\[ I'_{t+1} = \Pi_{I+\epsilon}\big(I_t + \alpha \cdot \mathrm{sign}(\nabla_I L(I_t, y))\big) \]
2

Patch-level contrastive loss: keep fine detail

Both images are split into non-overlapping \(K\times K\) patches. Each clean patch embedding \(z_i\) is aligned with its adversarial counterpart \(z_i'\), so local detail survives the perturbation. Standard contrastive losses need many negatives, and therefore large batches or a momentum encoder. SLADE avoids both with a stop-gradient on one side of each term:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}} = -\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\text{stopsim}(z_i, z_i') + \text{stopsim}(z_i', z_i)\Big), \quad \text{stopsim}(\cdot,\cdot) = \text{sim}(\cdot, \text{stopgrad}(\cdot)) \]
3

Global-level contrastive loss: keep overall meaning

Patch alignment alone can miss how patches relate to each other. A second term aligns the full-image embeddings \(Z\) and \(Z'\):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{global}} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\text{stopsim}(Z, Z') + \text{stopsim}(Z', Z)\Big) \]
4

Combine the two

A weight \(\lambda\) balances detail against overall meaning. \(\lambda = 0\) uses only the global term; larger values shift the focus to patches.

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{SLADE}} = \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}} + (1-\lambda)\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{global}}, \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1 \]
CLIP ViT-L/14 ImageNet, unlabeled λ = 0.6 patch size 32 batch size 64 2 epochs · AdamW SLADE2: ε = 2/255 SLADE4: ε = 4/255
Results

Robust against both exploits, with captions that keep their detail

SLADE is compared with the original CLIP and two state-of-the-art robust CLIP encoders, TeCoA and FARE, each trained at the same radius (superscript 2 = ε 2/255, 4 = ε 4/255). It is evaluated on OpenFlamingo-9B, LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-1.5-13B.
